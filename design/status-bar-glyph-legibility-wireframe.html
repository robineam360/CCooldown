Reading the level and the pace line at 14 dp

CCRM-48 (Status-Bar Gauge) shipped and is too small to read easily. This is the revision: five ways to spend the same slot, judged on one question — can you see how much is used, and where the pace line sits? No numbers anywhere; they are not the job of this glyph.

The measurement that reframes everything. The Fold 7 is 420 dpi (2.625×). UsageIcon.draw hands Android a 63 px = 24 dp bitmap and the status bar renders it at 37–38 px ≈ 14 dp — a 40% linear downscale that no app can opt out of, because the icon slot is system-fixed. Every "24 dp" number in the CCRM-48 wireframe was really 14 dp on glass, which is exactly why it read fine on the page and cramped on the phone.

So this page mocks at 14 dp, and shows the real 37-pixel raster. Where you see a magnified block below, it is the glyph rasterised to 37 px — the actual pixels the phone lights up — and then blown up. It is not a clean vector zoom, because a clean vector zoom is what misled us last time.

4 · The states that aren't the happy path

No data draws a bare track — never a full glyph, never a fake 0%. No reset clock means no pace line at all, never a guessed one. At 100% the fill closes and a notch opens, because a tinted mask has no colour to turn red with.

Why bars win on arithmetic, before taste comes into it. A ring asks you to judge where an arc ends as it travels around a circle; a bar asks you to judge one edge along a straight run. At this size the bar also gets a longer run — 14 dp of travel for A2 versus 8.5 dp of diameter on the shipped inner ring — and its pace line is a straight rule crossing the whole bar rather than a 1.5 dp nick in a thin band. Bars are also already this app's status-bar idiom: ic_stat_bars is the small icon for the alert and update notifications, and CCRM-43 (Bar Pace Marks) put pace marks on bars everywhere else. Rings are the odd one out.

7 · Two things I tested on the phone, and both changed the design

Rev A guessed at these. I built a probe — a 48 × 24 dp bitmap with a measuring frame, pure red/green/blue stripes and an asymmetric ring — installed it, and measured what the Fold 7 actually did with it. Both answers were surprises, in opposite directions.

7a · C2's wide bitmap — it does not work

The probe in the real status bar, magnified 6× — 48 × 24 dp of bitmap

Aspect ratio is honoured — the frame is a clean rectangle and the ring is circular, not an ellipse. But look at the size.

Measured: 39 × 20 px = 14.9 × 7.6 dp. The square icon measured 37 × 37 px = 14 × 14 dp. So the status bar allots a fixed-width slot of about 15 dp and fits the bitmap inside it preserving aspect — meaning a 2:1 bitmap buys the same width and loses half the height. Two rings side by side in that strip would be ~7 dp each: no better than option C, and worse than the square.

So C2 is dead, and the square bitmap is optimal — any non-square shape simply throws away part of the slot. My rev A speculation that it would be "fitted by height" was wrong; sorry for the detour, but this is exactly why it needed the test rather than the paragraph.

7b · Colour — it works in the status bar, and not in the tile

The probe's stripes are pure #FF0000, #00FF00, #0000FF. Sampling the screenshot pixel by pixel, the status bar reproduced them as exactly (255,0,0), (0,255,0), (0,0,255) — no tint, no wash, untouched. Android's documentation says small icons are alpha masks; Samsung's One UI plainly does not enforce it. CCRM-48's "the severity ladder cannot exist on this surface" was wrong on this hardware.

The same bitmap in the Quick Settings tile, magnified 3×

The identical red, green and blue stripes, rendered as three identical grey bars. The tile is a true alpha mask and flattens everything to one tint.

This asymmetry is the new design rule. One bitmap feeds both surfaces today, and they disagree about colour — so colour can only ever be an enhancement:
  • Alpha and shape must carry the level and the pace line, because that is all the tile will show — and all a stricter OEM would show either.
  • Colour carries severity and separates used from remaining, where the device allows it.
The clean way to get both is to stop sharing one bitmap: UsageIcon.draw takes a monochrome flag, the tile passes true and the notification passes false. Cheap, and each surface gets its best rendering.

7c · The colour design

Three elements, three deliberately different treatments — and the ranking survives when the colour is stripped, which is what keeps the tile honest.

ElementIn colour (status bar)Flattened (tile, or a stricter OEM)
Used the severity ladder — accent ■ · >80 ■ · >90 ■ · ≥100 ■ full-alpha ink
Remaining neutral at 26% — deliberately desaturated so the used arc pops ink at 26–34% — unchanged from today
Pace line ■ a cool hue (#5BC8FF) on a dark halo, overhanging both band edges the erased cut, exactly as shipped
Why a cool hue for the pace line. It can never collide with the warm severity ladder or the terracotta theme, so the line stays readable at every level — and it keeps the original contract that the pace mark answers "where am I in the window", never "how bad is it". Why it overhangs the band: that is the safety net. If a device does flatten the colour, the stretch crossing the fill disappears, but the two nubs sitting on the background still mark the position — the same trick the widget rings already use.

8 · Recommendation

  • Keep the square bitmap. Measured, not assumed: the slot is ~15 dp wide and fits by width, so anything non-square throws away height for nothing. C2 is dead.
  • Add the colour layer, with UsageIcon.draw taking a monochrome flag so the tile keeps the alpha-mask drawing and the status bar gets colour. This is the single biggest legibility win available — it separates used from remaining by hue as well as brightness, brings back the severity ladder that CCRM-48 (Status-Bar Gauge) had to abandon, and lets the pace line be a drawn cool-hued stroke rather than a 0.9 dp nick.
  • On shape, colour changes the maths. Hue does most of the work that size was failing to do, so the concentric rings become far more separable than they are today, and twin small rings become viable again. Compare 7c's rows before deciding — but my recommendation is still A2 stacked horizontal bars: it has the longest run for the level, the longest pace line, and it mirrors the notification body directly below it. In colour it is the clearest of the five at true size.
  • Whatever wins must survive flattening — judge it in the last board, not just the colourful one. That is the tile, and it is also every phone that enforces the alpha mask.
  • Not proposed: numbers, and the per-model third element — still out of scope.
Decisions — approved 2026-08-21, built and verified on the Fold 7 the same day:
  1. Square bitmap, one ring (option B), at 22.4 dp across on a 4 dp stroke — as large as the 24 dp box allows once the pace mark's overhang is accounted for. C2's wide bitmap was measured dead in §7a; the bars were not taken up.
  2. Colour, in the status bar only. Used wears the severity colour (the same Palette.barColor value as the notification's gauge), remaining is a mid neutral, and the pace mark is a cool #5BC8FF line.
  3. The mark stays an erased slot with the line inside it, the slot deliberately wider than the line — so a surface that flattens the bitmap still shows a gap. The overhang was cut right back after the first install: at this size a longer one reads as a spike into the ring's hollow rather than a mark on the band.
  4. The Quick Settings tile keeps the monochrome rendering, because it flattens colour anyway. UsageIcon.draw takes fillArgb; the tile passes null.
  5. The Twin style is withdrawn, and a stored "twin" migrates back to "ring".
Left open: the 7-day window is not on the glyph. A level for it costs the 5-hour ring its size — that is exactly what CCRM-48 (Status-Bar Gauge) proved unreadable. A state marker is a different proposition and is not yet drawn: a dot in the ring's hollow, shown only once the 7-day passes 80%, coloured by its rung where colour survives. It costs the 5-hour ring nothing because the hollow is already empty, it keeps the app's "colour appearing at all is the signal" habit, and it degrades honestly — on the tile the dot still means "the weekly is in trouble" without needing hue. Wireframe first if wanted.
