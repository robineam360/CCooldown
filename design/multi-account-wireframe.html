CCRM-6 · wireframe for approval

Multi-account — a dynamic profile registry

Profile stops being a two-constant enum and becomes a registry-backed value type: a stable string key, a never-reused slot int, and a user-editable label. Accounts become cards you add and remove. Two things are already settled and are not on the table here: the registry (not a third enum constant), and unconfigured profiles losing their tab. What follows is everything that looks different, in every state — including the ones nobody has looked at yet.

0 · The model, in one table

Not a visual, but every state below depends on it. Slots 0 and 1 stay pinned to personal/work: existing installs have placed widgets, armed alarms and posted notification IDs keyed off those ints. Slots come from a persisted monotonic counter and are never reused, so a stale widget or tile can never inherit a new account's data.

Rename and Remove both live behind the ⋮, deliberately away from Clear. Clear signs out and keeps the history (CCBG-1 (History Retention) made that call); Remove destroys it. Putting them side by side in the button row is a fat-finger disaster waiting to happen.

Remove is hidden on the last remaining card — the registry always holds at least one profile, which is what makes registry.first() total and keeps the zero-account fallback (state 5a) reachable without a synthetic ghost profile.

Tapping + Add account mints p2/slot 2 with the default label “Account 3” and appends the card in its familiar not-signed-in state — no name-first dialog. The user's next tap is Sign in on this phone, which is what they came for; renaming is a later, optional thought via ⋮ → Rename.

Default labels are “Account N” where N is the position in the list at creation time, not the slot — so the third account you add is “Account 3” even if you have removed one before. It is a starting label, not an identity; the key and slot are the identity.

2 · The “Profile names” section — recommend deleting it

Today there is a separate Profile names section below Accounts, with one text field per profile. Once names are registry-owned and each card can rename itself, that section is a second editor for the same field, and it has no way to grow a row for an account that does not exist yet.

Used everywhere — tabs, widgets, and notifications. Clear a field to go back to the default.

proposed — gone; the sentence moves into the rename dialog

Deleting it removes ~30 lines and one whole scroll-length of Settings, and it removes the “which of these two editors is authoritative” question entirely. The explanatory sentence (“Used everywhere — tabs, widgets, notifications”) is not lost; it moves into the rename dialog, where it is read at the moment it matters.

Cost: renaming becomes two taps instead of zero-taps-visible. That is the trade. If you would rather keep a single place to see all names at once, the alternative is keeping the section and having it iterate registry.all() — it grows a field per account and stays in sync, it is just redundant.

Decision 2 — does “Profile names” survive? Recommended: delete it, rename lives only on the card. Alternative: keep it, iterating the registry.

3 · Remove account — the only destructive path

Named in full, because it is the first caller HistoryStore.clear() and SessionLog.clear() have ever had, and because a year of session history is genuinely unrecoverable.

⋮ → Remove account

Remove “Teams”?

This deletes, permanently and only for this account:

  • the saved sign-in
  • its cached usage and settings — alerts, credits, reset pings
  • 8 days of trend history
  • a year of session and weekly history

Its Quick Settings tile goes blank, its launcher shortcut disappears, and any widget showing it switches to Personal. The pinned notification switches to Personal too.

To keep the history and just sign out, use Clear instead.

Because slots are never reused, there is no window in which a stale widget or a not-yet-removed tile can read a new account's data. The worst case is a surface showing nothing, which is correct.

Decision 3 — can Personal and Work be removed? Recommended: yes, any account except the last one. Their slots and keys are retired like any other, and the seed migration is gated on a one-time flag so a removed Personal does not resurrect on next launch. The alternative is making slots 0/1 permanent (Clear only), which is safer but means the person who never used “Work” is stuck with a dead card forever.

Same one-line fix (Row → FlowRow) for the three other non-wrapping chip rows: the widget config picker, the reset-ping Account row, and the debug endpoint probe.

Decision 5 — which pickers list unconfigured accounts? Recommended split: Settings pickers list every account (Show profile, reset pings, per-account alerts, credits — Settings is where accounts live, and you may want them set up before signing in), while content surfaces list only configured ones (tabs, History, widget picker, shortcuts). Alternative: configured-only everywhere, which makes the per-account alert toggles vanish for an account you have temporarily signed out of, losing the setting's visibility exactly when you are wondering why it went quiet.

6 · The pinned panel — “Huge number”, folding 2+ other accounts

Mocked in the Huge number style, the one actually in use. 8 · Quick Settings tiles — a fixed pool of four

A QS tile is a statically declared <service>; Android will not let the list be driven at runtime. So four tiles are declared, bound to slots 0–3. The two existing class names stay (renaming one breaks tiles already placed) and are rebound to slots 0 and 1.
