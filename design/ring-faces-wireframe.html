The two ring faces, small-first

Rev B, after review: the mini-rings face optimises for three rings, not four, and the single ring is designed for the small size it was always declared for rather than for a large placement. In both, the ring takes as much of the face as it can and the words move out of its way — the battery-gauge read.

Frames are drawn in dp at 2× and clip like real widgets. Ring geometry is the shipped RingRenderer, pace marks included (CCRM-43 (Bar Pace Marks)). The dashed red circle is the bore: the largest circle of clear space inside the stroke. Text crossing it is text the ring is crowding.

1 · CCBG-10 — mini-rings, tuned for three

The ring stops being a hardcoded 56 dp and takes the room the face has, up to 88 dp; one or two rings centre as a group instead of spreading to the quarter points. The row cap drops from four to three, so the widest case the layout has to serve is three — which is what lets the ceiling be this high.

2 · CCBG-11 — the single ring, at the size it's meant to be

The provider already declares this widget 2×2, minimum 110×110 dp (ring_widget_info.xml). Small is the intended shape, and the 178×240 dp placement is the outlier. That matters, because at the declared size today's four lines are far worse than what was reported: the bore holds about 57 dp of text and the stack needs roughly 71 dp. It doesn't crowd — it overflows.

2b · Proposed — the bore holds the number, and nothing else

One rule instead of a stack: the percentage lives inside the ring; every other word lives outside it. That's what lets the ring reach the edge of the face and stay legible at 110 dp. How much goes outside depends on the room — one line, two lines, or a column beside the ring on a wide short face.

3 · What rev B proposes

  • CCBG-10 · the ring takes the room, three-ring maximum. Diameter computed from the face instead of pinned at 56 dp, ceiling 88 dp; one or two rings centre as a group. windowRows gains a max parameter and the mini-rings face passes 3; its only other caller keeps 4, so nothing else moves.
  • CCBG-11 · the bore holds the percentage, full stop. Profile, countdown and exact reset all move outside the ring. That is what frees the ring to fill the face, and it is why this now works at 110 dp, where today's layout can't.
  • The ring is capped at 140 dp. Past that a solo ring stops being a gauge and becomes a poster. On a tall placement the extra height goes to the lines under it rather than to a bigger circle.
  • Both faces turn out to fix an overflow, not just an emptiness. At their declared minimums — 250×110 for mini-rings, 110×110 for the ring — today's layouts clip their own text. Neither had been looked at at those sizes.
  • Not changing: the pace tick and red segment, the severity ladder, the refresh glyph's corner, the stale pill, or what either face says when signed out.
To decide.
  1. The 140 dp ring cap — right ceiling, or should a tall placement just keep growing the circle?
  2. At 110 dp only the percentage and one line fit. Is the countdown the right survivor, or would you rather that line was the profile name on a two-account phone?
  3. Mini-rings at 88 dp — keep that ceiling, or let a one- or two-ring face go bigger still now that three is the maximum?
