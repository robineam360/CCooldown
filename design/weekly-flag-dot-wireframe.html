The weekly dot says pace, not level

Rev A keyed the dot on the 7-day's usage crossing 80%. Review redefined it: for a window a week long, nearness to the pace line matters more than the level — by the time a weekly crosses 80 it may be day 6 and there is nothing left to do about it, whereas "above pace on day 2" is the moment a correction is still cheap. Rev A's silence lasted exactly through the actionable period. Rev B fixes that.

Carried forward as already decided: dot diameter 7.5 dp (≈4.4 dp on screen), dead centre of the hollow, no dot when the weekly has no reading (honesty option A), Ring style only, no animation.

The strongest form of this idea: the dot is the app's pace sentence, drawn. The app already speaks a three-verdict pace vocabulary — "N points above even pace", "On even pace", "N points below even pace" — computed with the shared ±PACE_DEAD_ZONE (3 points) so surfaces can't flap or disagree. The dot's rungs bind to those verdicts exactly, plus one absolute rung on top:
DotPredicate (delta = used − elapsed)The sentence it mirrors
Red weekly used ≥ 100 (truncated; needs no reset clock) the window is spent — level, not pace
Yellow delta > 3"N points above even pace"
Grey −3 ≤ delta ≤ 3"On even pace"
No dotdelta < −3 "N points below even pace" — good news is silence
No new constants, no new thresholds: the dot goes yellow at the exact poll the in-app sentence flips to "above", so the two can never disagree. Review's "near the line but below" becomes the app's existing on-pace band, which includes a hair above the line — the same ±3 the sentence already treats as "on".
Dropping orange: agreed, with receipts. At 4.4 dp rendered, #FDD663 vs #FFA726 is a coin flip; and the Amber theme's accent is literally the yellow rung colour (both #FDD663/#F9A825), so warm-on-warm ambiguity already exists in this palette. Three rungs with three different meanings beat four hues two of which look alike. (The Amber quirk itself predates this work — noted in §6d.)

5 · Honesty rules

  • No weekly reading → no dot. Decided in rev A and unchanged: the dot is an alert, and no alerting system claims silence proves health. The panel one swipe away says "7-day no data" in words.
  • No reset clock → grey and yellow are impossible (there is no elapsed to compare against, and this app never guesses a pace). Red still fires, because it keys on the level alone. Shown in the ladder above.
  • No flicker games. The dot flips exactly when the pace sentence flips, on the same shared dead zone — consistency across surfaces over hysteresis on one of them. Flapping is already bounded by the ±3 band and the poll cadence.
  • One frequency consequence to accept knowingly: a steady user rides "on even pace" a lot, so the grey dot will be present more often than absent for them. That is the design: grey is deliberately the quietest mark — the loud claim is reserved for colour. If it proves noisy in practice, the fix is narrowing grey to a band strictly below the line, not widening the ladder.

6c · Why a true complement is off the table

Past 80 the fill is always the fixed warm ladder, whatever the theme — that override is the whole point of the ladder. A Blue theme's true complement is orange, and here is that line in the states where the glyph matters most:

Blue theme at 93% and 100% · each rule's line · 4×

Reading of 6b/6c. Rule X (true complement) dies exactly at 93 and 100 — a warm line on the warm ladder. Rule A (fixed blue) is fine on warm themes and merely mediocre on the blue family: position always survives (the erased slot guarantees that), hue coding doesn't. Rule B — a hand-tuned cool partner per theme — keeps hue coding alive everywhere: cyan-blue for the eight warm-or-neutral themes, spring green for Blue and Indigo, violet for Cyan and Teal. All three partners are cool, so all three survive the ladder override. Material You computes it: rotate the accent's hue 180°, then clamp out of the warm band the ladder owns. The partner lives as a third field on ThemeOption, next to the light/dark pair that already exists.

6d · Noted while here — the Amber quirk

Amber's accent equals the >80 yellow rung exactly, so on every bar in the app an Amber user cannot see the first ladder step (46% and 84% are the same colour). Pre-existing, palette-wide, not made worse by anything here — but if it deserves fixing, that is its own tracker item, not a rider on this one.

Decisions — approved 2026-08-21, built the same day:
  1. Grey binds to the on-pace verdict, |delta| ≤ PACE_DEAD_ZONE — the dot is the pace sentence, drawn; zero new constants (`RingGeometry.weeklyFlag`, pinned by `RingGeometryTest`). The frequency consequence in §5 accepted knowingly.
  2. Pace line: rule B — a cool partner per theme, as `paceLight`/`paceDark` on `ThemeOption`: cyan-blue for the eight warm-or-neutral themes, spring green for Blue/Indigo, violet for Cyan/Teal. Material You resolves through `byName` to Claude Orange on this surface, so its partner follows the same path — the computed-complement rule sketched in 6c is not needed until that surface learns true dynamic accents.
  3. Tile: grey at 45% alpha, yellow/red full — the rungs collapse to alpha, the message survives.
