CCRM-51 · Rails Gauge · rev J · as built

The Rails gauge on the status-bar icon

Your decisions are in: needle pinned at the hub, red slice over the severity fill, the four-rung 7-day dot whose no-usage rung is drawn empty (an outlined dot), and the 12 o'clock post gone below 100% but returning at 100% as the spent marker. Pie is a faint disc at Ring's footprint, the needle carries the Mac's own weight, and the marks are neutral ink — the gauges already spend their colour on the fill. Everything is drawn in a 24 dp box and rasterised to the real 37 px the status bar renders. Every question is answered; this is the as-built record.

Still nothing built. No Kotlin touched. Two of your answers reduce the work: "red slice over severity" means RingRenderer/BarRenderer are already correct and the widgets need no change at all.

3 · The 7-day dot — four rungs, as decided

Empty → grey → yellow → red. The no-usage rung is drawn as an outlined dot: empty is both what it means and how it looks. The moment any usage lands the dot fills — grey while there is nothing to flag, yellow above pace, red at a truncated 100. Because "empty" is a shape and not a colour, the whole ladder survives the Quick Settings tile, which throws colour away.

The grid below is the record of why literal black was ruled out, kept because it is the kind of thing that gets re-proposed later. Columns walk the weekly window, including the two states that need no clock at all and the one that draws nothing.

B1's second failure is the disqualifying one. On the Quick Settings tile Android throws the bitmap's colour away and tints every non-transparent pixel one colour — so a black filled dot arrives as a fully-inked dot, which in this ladder means spent. The rung meaning "you have used nothing this week" would render identically to "your week is gone" — an inversion, not just a loss. §12's middle row draws it. This is invisible from the Mac, whose template mode behaves differently, so it is not something their spec could have caught.
One thing this decision fixed for free. With "empty" now a rung of its own, no dot narrows to a single meaning: no weekly reading. That let me correct a real flaw in the earlier draft — a week with usage but no reset clock used to draw nothing, which under the new ladder would have falsely claimed "no reading". It now rests on grey: we know there is usage, so it is not empty, and we cannot claim it is above pace, so it is not yellow. Grey means "nothing to flag", which is exactly true. The no clock column above shows it.

4 · The 12 o'clock post as the spent marker

The post now earns its place by carrying one meaning only: this window is spent. Below 100 it does not draw, so the gauge stays as clean as you asked. At a truncated 100 it appears at 12 o'clock — and because it is a cleared halo with an ink line inside it, both the gap and the mark survive the Quick Settings tile's tinting, which a bare notch only half managed. Compare the 95% and 100% columns; the bottom row is the same gauge without it.

One edge case, drawn in the last column. At 100% the needle still draws, and if elapsed is near 0 or near 100 — a window burned almost instantly, or one about to reset — the needle lands at roughly 12 o'clock and merges with the post. The spent post is therefore drawn last, so the "spent" cue always wins the overlap. The two marks reading as one there is acceptable: at 100% used, the pace verdict is moot anyway.

One thing to look at before this is final: the >90 row. Orange #FFA726 fill with a red #FF5252 slice is the tightest pair in the ladder, and at 37 px on a dark bar they are close. 7 · The pie — decided: faint disc at Ring's footprint

An 18% ink disc behind the wedge, at Ring's own 11.2 dp radius. The four combinations stay below as the record — note how much smaller the Mac's Ø/2−1 inset rows render, and how much thinner the hairline rows are in the no data column. 14 · The record — and what is still unverified

Q1 (the no-usage rung) is settled — outlined "empty". Recorded in §3, along with why literal black was rejected and the no-clock correction it surfaced.
Q1 is settled — the 12 o'clock post returns at 100% and only there, replacing the notch I had proposed. Recorded in §4.
Q1 and Q2 are settled — the Mac's needle weight (§6) and the faint-disc pie at Ring's footprint (§7). Both sections keep their comparison grids as the record.
Nothing is open. Every question in this document is answered and the design is built — ui/UsageIcon.kt (drawing) and ui/RingGeometry.kt (the rung ladder, pure and unit-tested). The comparison grids above are kept deliberately: they are the record of what was ruled out and why, so the same proposals do not come back around.

One thing still outstanding: this has not been verified on the Fold 7 — no device was reachable when it was built. Every number here is rasterised to the real 37 px, which is what caught CCRM-48 (Status-Bar Gauge)'s failure, but that is not the same as looking at the phone. The states worth checking first are no data (the hairline alone, at 50%), the empty rung on a light bar, and 100% — the spent post over a closed red ring.

15 · Accessibility — unchanged

The gauge says nothing to a screen reader; the notification's content text carries every window in words, with "no data" and "not signed in" spelled out. None of these decisions touch that, and Q1's answer does not either — the dot never was the carrier.
